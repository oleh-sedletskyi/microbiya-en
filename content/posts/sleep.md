#  Why Sleep Is Our Most Important “Vitamin”

The cost of sleep deprivation is far greater than simply feeling tired. Sleep is a fundamental biological necessity that has a profound impact on our well-being, safety, and overall health.

Staying up late to finish urgent work or catch up on the news at the expense of sleep is definitely not the best idea. Here are 5 reasons why.

**Insufficient sleep reduces alertness and is independently associated with an increased risk of motor vehicle crashes.**

[Compared ](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29554902/)with sleeping 7–8 hours per night, sleeping 6 hours was associated with a 33% higher risk of motor vehicle crashes.

**Our immune system [depends](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/1032061) heavily on both the quantity and quality of sleep**

People who habitually sleep fewer than 6 hours per night are more than 4 times as [likely to develop]([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531403/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531403/) a common cold after viral exposure than those who sleep 7 hours or more.
Insufficient sleep also can impair [antibody production](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30920354/) and reduce immune responses to infection and vaccination.

**Sleep is critically important for emotional stability, cognitive performance, and decision-making**

During sleep, the brain consolidates newly acquired information and memories. [Insufficient sleep is associated with impaired learning and memory]([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23589831/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23589831/)), and increases the risk of anxiety and depression. Individuals with insomnia had a more than [twofold increased risk of developing depression]([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21300408/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21300408/)) compared with people without insomnia.

**Adequate sleep is essential for maintaining a healthy body weight**

Short sleep duration is associated with increased obesity risk. Meta-analyses show that adults with insufficient sleep have about 25–45% [higher odds of obesity]([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25450058/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25450058/)) compared with those sleeping a normal duration.

Sleep deprivation [raises levels of ghrelin]([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15583226/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15583226/)) (the hunger hormone) while lowering levels of leptin (the satiety hormone). It also reduces cellular sensitivity to insulin, which contributes to the development of metabolic disorders.

**Lack of sleep accelerates aging and contributes to visible signs of aging**

Studies have shown that [sleep deprivation impairs the skin’s ability to recover]([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25266053/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25266053/) from environmental stressors such as UV exposure. Shorter sleep duration has also been [associated]([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3199186/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3199186/)) with accelerated telomere shortening, one of the biological markers of aging.

So, try not to neglect your sleep—and stay healthy!
